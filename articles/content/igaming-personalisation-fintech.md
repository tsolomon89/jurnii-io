# What iGaming’s Personalisation Experiment Can Teach Fintech

By Fraser Dunk, CEO

For years, the financial technology sector has looked to traditional banking for its comparative benchmarks. Fintech disruptors won early market share by offering cleaner interfaces, faster account setup, and lower fees than legacy financial institutions. Today, however, that initial design advantage has eroded. As mobile banking becomes commoditised, Fintech operators face the same retention challenges that have defined the iGaming sector for a decade: high customer multi-homing, zero switching costs, and rising customer acquisition costs (CAC).
To find the next level of user retention, Fintech product teams must look beyond their own industry boundaries. They must study the iGaming sector, which runs some of the most complex, real-time personalization campaigns in the world. Driven by the reality that 70% to 80% of players hold 3 to 5 active accounts simultaneously, sportsbooks and casino operators have developed highly agile front-end architectures that adapt dynamically to user behavior in milliseconds. Unpacking these cross-industry lessons reveals how Fintech can optimize its customer journeys, balance compliance against usability, and build digital trust.

## The iGaming Personalization Engine: Dynamic menu structures and Live Feeds

The iGaming personalization model is built on real-time adaptation. When a user opens a sports betting application, they are not presented with a static, generic dashboard. Instead, the interface adapts based on their location, past betting history, preferred teams, and the current sporting calendar.
During a live football match, the sports lobby updates dynamically, prioritizing in-play betting markets and live odds relevant to the player's profile. This personalization requires a high-performance data architecture that bridges backend transaction databases—known as Player Account Management (PAM) systems—and front-end presentation layers in near-real-time.
Fintech applications, by contrast, remain static. A user opening a retail investment or digital banking app is typically presented with the same layout, regardless of whether they are a high-net-worth investor, a daily budgeter, or a casual saver. The interface fails to adapt to their financial behaviors or current needs.
By adopting iGaming’s dynamic menu structures, Fintech platforms can present relevant services at the optimal moment. For example, a digital banking app could surface currency exchange tools when it detects a user has purchased international flights, or highlight retirement planning options when a user receives a salary increase. This dynamic prioritization reduces cognitive load, helps users find features, and increases cross-selling efficiency.

## Resolving the Compliance and KYC Onboarding Bottleneck

Both iGaming and Fintech operate under strict regulatory frameworks. Both sectors must enforce Know Your Customer (KYC) guidelines, Anti-Money Laundering (AML) checks, and identity verification steps. These compliance checks represent the highest point of friction in the user journey, resulting in significant funnel leakage.
In Fintech, registration onboarding is often treated as a cold, compliance-driven process. Users are asked to enter extensive personal data, upload identity documents, and complete facial recognition scans within interfaces that feel detached from the main application. These secure forms often look generic and outdated, triggering user anxiety during high-stress verification steps.
iGaming sportsbooks face identical regulatory hurdles but have optimized these flows using proactive UX heuristics. They recognize that compliance does not justify a poor user experience. iGaming platforms guide users through verification using interactive, self-correcting forms, real-time verification indicators, and clear explanations of why specific data is required. When a passport scan fails, they do not just show a generic error; they provide immediate feedback on lighting or cropping to prevent frustration and abandonment.
Furthermore, iGaming platforms integrate trust signals directly into high-anxiety moments. By ensuring aesthetic consistency across registration, identity upload, and cashier interfaces, they reassure players that their sensitive data is secure. Fintech platforms must apply these design patterns to their onboarding funnels, transforming KYC from a compliance obstacle into an interactive user guide that builds brand trust.

## The Commercial Cost of Performance Friction

In digital products, performance is a commercial metric. In iGaming, where players place bets during live sporting events, a delay of a single second can mean the difference between a completed transaction and a lost customer. Jurnii’s data shows that a 1.2-second delay on a deposit screen directly correlates with transaction abandonment and lost Net Gaming Revenue (NGR).
This performance standard is often ignored in Fintech. Banking applications frequently suffer from slow API response times, layout shifts during login, and processing delays during fund transfers. Product teams often assume that because users are managing their money, they will tolerate slow performance.
This assumption is a commercial liability. As alternative financial apps emerge, multi-homing users will defect to the platform that provides the lowest transaction friction. Fintech product teams must prioritize technical front-end performance, treating Core Web Vitals, page-load speed, and device responsiveness as non-negotiable conversion targets. Just as Jurnii UX audits iGaming platforms to isolate performance bottlenecks, Fintech operators must continuously monitor their technical speed across devices and regions to protect their acquisition spend.

## Building an Intelligence Moat: The Power of Continuous Benchmarking

The personalization models developed by iGaming operators were not built on gut feel or static audit reports. They are the result of continuous competitive benchmarking and data analysis. Operators track competitor promotion types, release frequencies, and homepage Share of Voice (SoV) daily to optimize their own propositions.
Fintech must adopt this always-on intelligence strategy. Operating on internal analytics alone creates a strategic blind spot. To optimize acquisition and retention, Fintech teams must monitor the wider market context. They must understand how competitors are structuring their sign-up incentives, how quickly they verify identity documents, and where usability friction exists in their registration flows.
By integrating automated competitor tracking and journey benchmarking into their workflows, Fintech operators can build a defensible intelligence moat. This data-driven approach allows product and marketing teams to make decisions based on objective market context, ensuring their platform remains competitive.
The personalization experiment in iGaming has proven that in a multi-homing market, user experience is the ultimate defensive asset. By adopting the real-time agility, compliance guidance, and performance standards of iGaming, Fintech platforms can close the execution gap, protect their margins, and secure customer loyalty.
