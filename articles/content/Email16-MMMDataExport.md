# Why Promo Intelligence Belongs Inside MMM

By Mitch Vidler, CCO

The iGaming industry is undergoing a quiet revolution in marketing measurement. As privacy regulations tighten, third-party cookies face deprecation, and mobile tracking restrictions limit the effectiveness of multi-touch attribution (MTA), operators are returning to Marketing Mix Modelling (MMM). Modern machine-learning-based MMM frameworks allow marketing teams to calculate channel-level return on investment (ROI) by analysing aggregate historical data.
However, a major structural flaw exists in how sportsbooks and casinos implement these models. Traditional MMM is designed to analyze marketing effectiveness in a vacuum. It ingests internal variables, including media spend, search impressions, creative cycles, and internal promotion schedules, while assuming that consumer demand responds only to these inputs.
This assumption is false. In the highly competitive iGaming sector, player behavior is shaped by external market forces. Jurnii’s data shows that 70% to 80% of UK players hold 3 to 5 active operator accounts simultaneously. These players are price-sensitive and switch between apps in seconds. If a competitor launches a highly generous promotion, it will immediately suppress your conversion rates, regardless of your campaign's creative strength or bid optimization. Without integrating competitor promotional intelligence into your models, your marketing mix modelling will misread performance and misallocate budgets.

## The Distortion of Competitor Noise

In marketing attribution, competitor promotions represent a form of exogenous noise that distorts performance signals. When a competitor launches a highly aggressive promotional campaign, they temporarily absorb player attention and wallet share.
Consider a common scenario: a sportsbook increases its paid search and paid social media spend by 15% ahead of a major international fixture, expecting a proportional increase in first-time deposits (FTDs). Instead, the campaign's conversion rate drops by 20%, and the cost per acquisition (CPA) rises.
Without competitor context, a traditional MMM framework will attribute this decline to internal campaign inefficiencies. The model may conclude that the creative assets have reached saturation, that bid strategies were set too high, or that paid search has reached a point of diminishing returns. Based on these outputs, the marketing team might decide to decrease spend in this channel for future fixtures.
The actual cause of the performance decline was external. During the fixture build-up, two major competitors launched market-leading price boosts on high-profile player props, such as "Harry Kane to score anytime," and promoted them heavily across their homepages and social channels. These competitor boosts carried a high Promo Richness Index, conceding substantial margin to the player. Multi-homing bettors naturally directed their deposits to the sportsbooks offering the highest true value.
Your paid search campaign did not fail because of poor execution; its performance was temporarily suppressed by competitor pricing aggression. By failing to account for this external noise, your model draws the wrong conclusion, leading to strategic mistakes that can damage acquisition volumes.

## The Three Critical Competitive Variables for MMM

To resolve this attribution bias, operators must feed competitor promotion data directly into their causal models. Jurnii 360 tracks competitor activity daily across 21 feature areas, converting raw market noise into structured, MMM-ready variables. To build an accurate model, marketing teams must focus on three core competitor dimensions:
First, Promotional Volume. This metric tracks the absolute frequency and density of competitor campaign launches. Measuring competitor promotional frequency allows the model to isolate the baseline noise level in the market, establishing how active the competitive environment was during any given attribution window.
Second, Generosity (Promo Richness Index). Not all promotions carry the same weight. A 20% deposit bonus is structurally different from a 100% deposit match, and a boost on a single player prop is different from a boost on a complex bet builder. Jurnii’s proprietary Promo Richness Index (PRI) solves this by translating odds boosts and bonus structures back into implied probabilities, isolating the exact margin conceded by the competitor. Incorporating the PRI into your model provides a standardized measure of competitive pricing pressure.
Third, Creative cycles and Homepage Share of Voice (SoV). A promotion is only effective if players see it. Sportsbooks compete aggressively for the limited real estate on app store listings, homepage banners, and mobile push notifications. Jurnii 360 tracks the duration and prominence of competitor campaigns, calculating their homepage Share of Voice. This metric allows models to weight competitor promotions by their actual market visibility.

## Technical Integration: From Data Export to Causal Model

Integrating Jurnii 360 data into your marketing mix models is straightforward. Traditional MMM tools, such as Google’s LightweightMMM or Meta’s Robyn, rely on regression analysis to estimate the contribution of different marketing channels to a target metric, such as FTD volume or Net Gaming Revenue (NGR).
To integrate competitive intelligence, data science teams treat competitor Promo Richness Index and homepage Share of Voice metrics as exogenous control variables, alongside traditional controls like seasonality, inflation, and public holidays.
During model training, the regression algorithm estimates the coefficients for these competitor variables. Because competitive pricing aggression has a negative impact on internal conversions, the model will output negative coefficients for competitor PRI and SoV.
Once these competitor coefficients are calculated, the model can decompose conversion variance with higher accuracy. When a channel’s performance drops during a period of competitor aggression, the model attributes that drop to the competitor variable rather than the channel's baseline coefficient. This prevents the underestimation of your marketing effectiveness and ensures that your channel ROI calculations remain accurate. Jurnii Cortex automates this causal impact modelling, allowing teams to run scenario planning and predict how changes in competitor generosity will impact acquisition yields.
