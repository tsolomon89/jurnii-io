## The Commercial Cost of Blind Generosity

Promotions are the largest single variable expense for online gaming operators. In highly competitive markets, operators routinely spend between 30% and 40% of their gross gaming revenue (GGR) on player incentives. Yet, much of this investment is inefficient. 
When you design a promotion without objective market context, you are operating in a blind spot. If you offer a signup bonus that is 20% more generous than the active market average, you are overpaying for acquisition. You write high volumes of first-time deposits (FTDs), but the revenue generated is unprofitable. 
Conversely, if your promotions are 10% less generous than the market set, your acquisition volumes decline. Players, who hold 3 to 5 active accounts, recognize the better value elsewhere and shift their wallet share. 
To prevent these outcomes, your commercial team must have access to near-real-time competitor data. You need to know when competitors launch promotions, which player segments they target, and how generous their offers are. The Promo Richness Index provides this clarity, converting unstructured competitor activity into an active commercial asset.

## The Mathematics of the Promo Richness Index

Measuring promotional generosity is complex. You cannot simply compare headline bonus values. A competitor offering a £50 deposit bonus is not necessarily more generous than an operator offering a £20 bonus. 
To determine the true commercial value of a promotion, you must analyze the underlying terms. 
The Promo Richness Index calculates generosity by factoring in multiple variables:
- **Headline value:** The maximum bonus size or free bet amount.
- **Wagering requirements:** The multiplier determining how many times a player must turn over the bonus before withdrawing funds.
- **Minimum odds:** The threshold odds required to qualify for sports promotions.
- **Expiry times:** The duration the player has to meet the wagering criteria.
- **Targeting rules:** Whether the offer is restricted to specific player segments, game categories, or deposit methods.
The index acts as a translation layer. It parses these complex rules and converts them into a single, standardized score. This score represents the true value delivered to the player. 
By tracking this index daily across 35 markets, Jurnii 360 allows you to compare your propositions directly against your competitors. You can see, for example, that your slots promotions score 65/100, while your primary competitor scores 55/100. This data shows that you are overpaying for slots players, allowing you to adjust your wagering requirements to protect your casino margins.

## Optimizing Sportsbook Spend: Protecting the Hold Rate

Sportsbook operators operate on extremely tight margins. The popularity of live in-play betting has increased the frequency of promotional offers. During major sporting events, competitors launch dozens of daily boosts, enhanced multiples, and event-specific free bets. 
Many trading and marketing teams respond to these campaigns by matching competitor boosts. They increase their odds to match the highest value in the market. 
This reactionary approach erodes your hold rate. You convert bets, but the liability increases, diluting your trading margins. 
By using the Promo Richness Index, sportsbooks can optimize their promotional spend. You can monitor competitor boost frequencies and values in near-real-time. 
If Jurnii 360 shows that your football offers score 75/100 while your competitors average 60/100, you have room to adjust. You can reduce your odds boosts slightly to protect your hold rate. 
Conversely, if the index shows you are lagging behind on horse racing, you can increase your generosity in that specific vertical to capture active players. This targeted allocation protects your overall margins while maintaining competitiveness in key markets.

## Managing Casino Margins: The Wagering Requirement Lever

Casino players are highly sensitive to wagering ratios. In slots and live casino games, the wagering requirement is the primary driver of player perception. A player will choose a 10x wagering requirement on a £10 bonus over a 45x requirement on a £50 bonus because the probability of withdrawal is significantly higher. 
Many operators attempt to protect their casino margins by setting high wagering requirements across all promotions. This strategy is counterproductive. It drives players to competitors who offer lower wagering requirements. 
With Jurnii 360, casino operators can monitor competitor wagering ratios across slots, roulette, and blackjack daily. 
If the index shows that your competitors are offering 20x wagering requirements on slots while you demand 40x, your offer is uncompetitive. 
Instead of increasing the headline bonus value—which is expensive—you can optimize the wagering multiplier. By lowering your wagering requirement to 25x, you increase your Promo Richness Index score, making the offer competitive without increasing the absolute bonus liability. This structured optimization protects your margin while driving player retention.
