# The CFO's Guide to UX: Connecting Usability Metrics to Net Gaming Revenue

By Mitch Vidler, CCO

For the Chief Financial Officer, user experience (UX) has historically been viewed as a soft, aesthetic cost center. When product and design teams request capital to fund interface redesigns or new front-end development, they often present their proposals using subjective, non-financial terminology. They argue that a new layout will make the application feel more modern, or that it will make the registration flow more engaging. To a CFO focused on margin protection, rising taxes, and capital allocation, these aesthetic arguments carry little weight. 
Design changes must not be evaluated on visual appeal. They must be measured by their direct impact on the balance sheet. 
In the online gaming industry, where player acquisition costs (CAC) are rising and retention is low, usability is a primary driver of financial performance. 
Under the Jurnii UX framework, we translate experience metrics directly into financial Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). We connect page load speed, input error rates, and security placement to first-time deposits (FTDs), hold rates, and Net Gaming Revenue (NGR). 
By grounding your user experience decisions in financial reality, you convert design from a speculative expense into a measurable revenue driver, ensuring that every engineering hour invested delivers a clear, quantifiable return.

## The Financial Language of Experience

The primary obstacle to experience optimization is a communication gap. Product design teams and finance departments speak entirely different languages. Designers focus on components, typography, layout consistency, and user satisfaction scores. CFOs focus on NGR, return on asset (ROA), cash flow, and player lifetime value (LTV). 
This division creates an operational bottleneck. Product teams fail to secure budget for critical usability upgrades because they cannot prove the financial cost of friction. 
Jurnii UX bridges this gap by acting as a translation layer. We analyze your digital experience across 21 distinct feature areas, converting usability findings into a standardized financial index. 
Instead of reporting that "the deposit page is confusing," Jurnii UX quantifies the deposit completion ratio. 
We show that a validation error on the credit card input field is causing a 12% abandonment rate among high-value players. 
By placing a financial value on this friction point—calculating the exact NGR lost from abandoned deposits—we give the product team the commercial visibility needed to justify optimization spend. Experience optimization becomes a structured project with a clear, board-defensible return on investment (ROI).

## Attributing Net Gaming Revenue to Usability Performance

To attribute NGR growth directly to usability improvements, you must apply a structured financial methodology. Jurnii UX isolates external market variables to measure the exact revenue recovered from removing design friction. 
Consider the registration funnel. If your marketing team drives 10,000 prospective players to your registration landing page monthly, but your registration completion rate is only 40%, you are leaking 6,000 potential accounts. 
If Jurnii UX audits reveal that 20% of this abandonment occurs at the postcode input field due to a slow, manual verification API, the cost of that validation friction is clear: 1,200 lost accounts monthly. 
If your average first-time depositor (FTD) delivers an initial value of £50, this single usability flaw is costing your business £60,000 in monthly FTD volume. 
By implementing Jurnii Studio's optimized address autocomplete component, you resolve this validation barrier. 
If the registration completion rate subsequently rises from 40% to 45%, you acquire an additional 500 depositors monthly. This shift generates £25,000 in incremental monthly revenue, directly attributable to the interface optimization. 
By applying this financial math to every step of the player journey, you can see exactly where friction is leaking revenue and how much NGR your optimization team recovers.

## The Cost of Latency in the Transactional Funnel

Technical performance is another area where minor design choices have severe financial consequences. Many operators treat page load speed as a pure technical metric rather than a conversion driver. 
However, in transaction-heavy funnels like sports betting betslips or payment confirmation screens, speed is directly linked to transaction success. 
Jurnii’s database shows that any latency over our 1.2-second performance threshold correlates with immediate transaction abandonment. 
For every 0.1-second delay in page rendering or button responsiveness on a payment confirmation screen, deposit success rates drop by 1.8%. 
This latency drop directly compresses your net gaming revenue. 
If your payment confirmation screen takes 2.5 seconds to process—1.3 seconds over the threshold—you are suffering a 23.4% reduction in deposit completion rates. For an operator processing £1,000,000 in monthly deposits, this latency is leaking over £230,000 in potential player funds. 
Optimizing your front-end code, compressing assets, and streamlining payment scripts are not just maintenance tasks. They are direct revenue protection strategies. Jurnii UX tracks these performance metrics daily, showing your executive team the exact cost of technical debt.
