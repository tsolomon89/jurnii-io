## Five Ways AI Is Transforming Commercial Decision-Making in Regulated iGaming

### Domain-specific intelligence platforms replace reactive analytics with continuous market benchmarking across product, promotional, and marketing operations.

**LONDON, UK — July 23, 2026** — Artificial intelligence is now embedded across five key operational areas in regulated iGaming, shifting operators from reactive reporting toward continuous, proactive intelligence.

As platform commoditisation and rising Cost Per Acquisition (CAC) squeeze operational margins, iGaming executive teams rely on domain-specific AI infrastructure to protect Net Gaming Revenue (NGR) and maintain strategic differentiation.

[Jurnii](https://jurnii.io), the commercial intelligence layer for iGaming, outlines the five core applications of AI currently driving commercial decision-making:

1. **Continuous UX benchmarking:** Domain-specific AI models audit digital user journeys across 300+ competing operator brands simultaneously. [Jurnii UX](https://jurnii.io/products/ux) evaluates journey effectiveness, usability, performance, and perception, delivering 70+ commercially weighted recommendations per audit.
2. **Automated competitor proposition tracking:** Machine learning systems monitor competitor activity in near-real-time. [Jurnii 360](https://jurnii.io/products/360) tracks 400+ monthly promotions and 5,000+ odds boosts across the UK market alone, capturing competitor moves across 21 feature areas.
3. **Causal marketing attribution:** Econometric Marketing Mix Modelling (MMM) replaces last-click models by integrating competitor generosity and UX friction scores. [Jurnii Cortex](https://jurnii.io/products/cortex) delivered a +46% marketing efficiency improvement and identified $4.85M in budget reconciliation opportunities across a $17.1M engagement.
4. **Predictive scenario planning:** Causal AI engines simulate the revenue impact of spend reallocations, competitor promotional adjustments, and product changes before operators commit capital.
5. **Proactive churn prevention:** Automated models pinpoint specific payment and registration friction points that cause the 10% of high-value players who generate 80% of revenue to switch to competing platforms.

"The operators building durable competitive advantage invest in proprietary, domain-specific intelligence infrastructure," said Fraser Dunk, Chief Executive Officer at Jurnii. "Generic AI tools create operational parity across competitors. Structured, domain-specific intelligence creates commercial separation. In a market where 70–80% of players hold accounts with multiple operators, leadership teams must base strategic decisions on continuous market evidence rather than internal opinion."

### Commercial Intelligence Applications Summary

To deploy AI effectively across regulated operations, operators unify five distinct application mechanisms within a single commercial intelligence architecture:

- **UX Benchmarking (Jurnii UX):** Uses 4-dimension journey auditing to identify registration, deposit, and cashout friction before it affects conversion rates.
- **Competitor Tracking (Jurnii 360):** Automates the capture of headline promotions, bonus mechanics, and odds boosts across 35 markets daily.
- **Marketing Attribution (Jurnii Cortex):** Replaces last-click attribution with causal Marketing Mix Modelling (MMM) calibrated against competitor generosity.
- **Scenario Planning (Jurnii Cortex):** Executes financial simulations to predict how budget shifts and competitor moves alter Net Gaming Revenue (NGR).
- **Churn Prevention (Jurnii UX):** Pinpoints friction points in retention journeys that cause high-value players to switch to competing platforms.

## How is AI used in iGaming?

AI is used in regulated iGaming across five primary commercial functions: automated UX benchmarking, real-time competitor proposition tracking, causal marketing attribution (MMM), predictive scenario planning, and proactive churn prevention. Rather than generic language models, operators deploy domain-specific AI infrastructure—such as the Jurnii commercial intelligence layer—to convert unstructured market data into evidence-backed decisions that grow Net Gaming Revenue (NGR).

## Why does domain-specific AI outperform generic tools in regulated markets?

Generic AI models and general-purpose LLMs generate text and basic code, but lack the structural data architecture required for enterprise commercial decisions in regulated iGaming:

- **Regulatory Compliance Context:** Regulated iGaming requires AI models calibrated for jurisdiction-specific KYC, AML, and responsible gambling constraints across the UK, EU, and North America.
- **Commercial Weighting:** Domain-specific AI links visual and promotional data directly to financial metrics including Net Gaming Revenue (NGR), Player Lifetime Value (LTV), and hold rate.
- **Proprietary Data Moats:** Multi-product intelligence models trained on longitudinal datasets compound in value, whereas generic AI outputs create parity across competing operators.
