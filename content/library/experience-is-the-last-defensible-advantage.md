# Experience is the Last Defensible Advantage

By Fraser Dunk, CEO

The iGaming market has reached a point of structural commoditisation. In the early days of online gaming, operators competed on technology, licensing, and access. Today, those barriers have dissolved. Most operators run their businesses on the same B2B platform stacks. They license the same game libraries from the same major studios. They match the same sports betting odds from the same global feeds. They copy each other's promotional mechanics within hours of launch. 
If five competing operators run on the same underlying technology stack, they are not competing on product. They are competing on a reskin. Differentiation cannot wait on vendor roadmaps, which are notoriously slow and prioritize group-level requirements over local market needs. In this environment, where the core proposition is identical, the player's decision to stay, return, or defect is made entirely at the level of digital experience. 
Experience is the last defensible moat. It is the only variable within an operator's direct control that cannot be easily copied by a competitor.

## The Multi-Homing Reality

Player loyalty in online gaming is extremely fragile. The modern player does not restrict themselves to a single brand. Jurnii’s market data shows that 70% to 80% of UK players hold 3 to 5 active operator accounts simultaneously. They keep these apps installed on their mobile devices, funded, and ready to use. 
The cost of switching between these accounts is zero. The decision to switch is made in seconds. If a player encounters a moment of friction on your platform, they do not file a support ticket. They do not wait for your product team to release a fix in the next sprint. They simply close your application and open a competitor’s app. 
This switching behavior is particularly acute among your most valuable customers. The 10% of players who drive 80% of operator net gaming revenue (NGR) are the most likely to multi-home. These high-value players interact with digital betting products with high frequency. This frequency makes them highly sensitive to even minor usability issues. 
Many operators attempt to mitigate this with VIP relationship managers or manual loyalty bonuses. However, even the most attentive VIP manager cannot overcome a broken deposit screen or a verification block at the moment of a major sporting event. When your registration flow lags, or your deposit screen takes too long to load, you are not just losing a casual session. You are risking the defection of a high-value customer. When you lose these players, you lose your core revenue stream. Protecting this wallet share requires a deep, continuous understanding of the experience you deliver relative to the active market.

## The Cost of Reactive Analytics

Most operators fail to measure experience objectively. They operate under the illusion of awareness, relying on manual reviews, internal gut feel, or quarterly agency audits. These methods are slow, subjective, and expensive. 
Furthermore, traditional analytics platforms are reactive. They explain the past. They tell you that your conversion rate dropped last month, or that player churn increased last quarter. They require you to lose hundreds of players and significant revenue before a statistical trend becomes visible in your dashboards. 
Traditional funnel tracking might show that a player abandoned their session at the deposit stage. It cannot tell you why they abandoned it. It cannot tell you that the player left because the payment methods were not sorted by regional popularity, or because the deposit form lacked clear security indicators. By the time this friction shows up in your database, the commercial damage is already done. You have already lost the player, the First Time Deposit (FTD), and the customer lifetime value. You cannot recover that spend. 
To protect your NGR, you must identify friction before it causes player defection. You need proactive UX intelligence. ## Cumulative Friction: The Silent Killer

Experience failure is rarely the result of a single, catastrophic system outage. Instead, it is the result of cumulative friction. It is the weight of multiple minor issues that build up across a player journey. 
It is a 1.2-second delay on a deposit screen. It is an identity verification flow that fails on an edge case. It is a promotional banner that appears at the wrong moment of decision, interrupting the user's flow. It is a betslip that requires three clicks instead of one. 
Individually, these issues seem minor. Product managers often dismiss them as low priority. Collectively, however, they compound. They introduce hesitation, erode brand trust, and interrupt the player's momentum.

Relying on quarterly snapshots or manual competitor sweeps leaves massive competitive blind spots. The iGaming market moves daily. Banners change. Onboarding flows are updated. Competitors launch new promotional mechanics and boosts.
