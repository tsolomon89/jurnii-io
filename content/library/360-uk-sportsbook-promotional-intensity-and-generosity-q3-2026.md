## Executive summary

The introduction of the UK's 40% Remote Gaming Duty has compressed operator gross gaming revenue margins across sports betting and igaming. In response, promotional strategy has divided sharply: while some operators have instituted disciplined staking caps, major heritage brands have accelerated promotional frequency and price boosting to defend market share.

This report delivers an empirical benchmark of promotional intensity, boost volume, and market richness across 24 regulated UK operators tracked by the Jurnii 360 platform in September 2026. The findings illustrate a pronounced concentration of promotional spend and enhanced pricing:

- **Total Tracked Promotional Value**: **£26,820,949** across **315** active promotional campaigns (+3.0% period-on-period).
- **Market Richness Index**: **3,288.5%**, reflecting extensive promotional diversity and multi-channel campaign deployment.
- **Boost Dominance**: Across **1,117** active price and odds boosts, three operators – **William Hill (368)**, **Ladbrokes (230)**, and **Bet365 (198)** – account for **71.3%** of the entire UK market.
- **William Hill's Single-Brand Hegemony**: William Hill alone commands **32.9%** of all active boosts in the UK market, operating at more than triple the boost volume of digital challengers such as Midnite (101).
- **Campaign Volume Leader**: **Ladbrokes** leads the market in promotional campaign frequency with **29** active sports campaigns and **75** total promotional offers detected over a 24-hour cycle.

## The boost economy: 71.3% concentration across three operators

Odds and price boosts have evolved into the primary real-time engagement mechanism for UK sportsbook operators. With 1,117 active boosts observed across the market, boost volume is concentrated within an oligopoly:

1. **William Hill (368 boosts, 32.9% share)**: William Hill treats price boosting as an ambient merchandising layer. Boosts span virtually all Premier League and Champions League fixtures, horse racing meetings, and niche US sports. The operator's strategy relies on volume: providing bettors with an enhanced price on nearly every prominent event to simulate market-leading value.
2. **Ladbrokes (230 boosts, 20.6% share)**: Ladbrokes focuses boosts heavily on football multiples and horse racing daily specials, integrating them directly into carousel navigation on the mobile homepage.
3. **Bet365 (198 boosts, 17.7% share)**: Bet365 deploys boosts selectively on key match markets (First Goalscorer, Bet Builder enhancements), maintaining strict maximum stake limits (£10–£20) to manage liability.

Together, these three brands account for **796 of the 1,117 boosts** active in the market (**71.3%**). By contrast, Betfair Sportsbook (25 boosts) and Paddy Power (66 boosts) take a significantly more restrained approach, relying on exchange pricing and brand marketing rather than widespread retail price inflation.

## Bonus mechanics and vertical distribution

Analysis of promotional mechanics reveals how UK operators allocate bonus capital between acquisition and retention:

### 1. Mechanic breakdown across active promotions
- **Deposit Bonuses (15 campaigns)**: Primarily utilized in casino cross-sell and high-value VIP re-engagement.
- **Free Bets (14 campaigns)**: Standardised wagering concessions distributed via loyalty clubs (e.g. Sky Bet Club).
- **Enhanced Odds (14 campaigns)**: Branded price enhancements tied to specific television matches.
- **Bet & Get (12 campaigns, 13.4% of catalog)**: The dominant structural mechanic in UK sports betting. Staking thresholds typically require £5–£10 to unlock £20–£40 in non-withdrawable free bets, enforcing an initial wagering commitment before capital release.
- **Winnings Boosts (11 campaigns)**: Percentage boosts applied to winning accumulator payouts (ranging from 5% to 70% based on leg count).
- **Price Concessions (11 campaigns)**: Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG) and extra each-way place terms in racing.

### 2. Vertical and sport allocation
- **Football** accounts for **34.8% of all boost volume**, driven by Bet Builder combinations and enhanced match outcome doubles.
- **Horse Racing** dominates standard promotional campaigns (**41.0% share**), supported by daily place concessions, faller insurance, and non-runner money-back guarantees.

## Strategic implications for commercial and trading teams

For commercial leadership navigating the UK's duty-compressed landscape, the Jurnii 360 data indicates three critical strategic imperatives:

1. **Audit boost margin degradation**: Operators running hundreds of active boosts (such as William Hill and Ladbrokes) face severe margin compression during high-volume football weekends. Commercial teams must evaluate whether continuous price inflation cannibalises standard-margin wagering among existing sticky bettors.
2. **Counter volume with mechanic precision**: Mid-tier and challenger operators cannot match William Hill's 368 boosts without severe balance sheet exposure. Challengers must focus on mechanic innovation: personalised bet builder enhancements, automated early payouts, and low-friction withdrawal journeys that build retention without perpetual price subsidies.
3. **Harmonise promotional generosity with platform stability**: High-frequency promotional pushes create operational liabilities if the client-side user experience is slow. Offering an attractive Bet & Get promotion yields negative ROI if the prospective customer encounters registration latency or cashier friction.
