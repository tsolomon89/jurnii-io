Attributing marketing return on investment (ROI) in digital gaming has reached an existential crossroads. For over a decade, marketing directors, digital acquisition leads, and performance agencies relied on user-level tracking infrastructure: third-party cookies, Apple Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA), Google Advertising IDs (GAID), and deterministic multi-touch attribution (MTA) models.

Over the past three years, this deterministic tracking architecture has collapsed. The deprecation of third-party cookies across modern browsers, Apple App Tracking Transparency (ATT) framework, and strict global privacy regulations (GDPR in Europe, LGPD in Brazil, and state-level privacy mandates in North America) have severed the link between individual marketing impressions and player registrations.

Concurrently, many operators have retreated to rudimentary **last-click attribution** models within Google Analytics or internal BI tools. 

This retreat introduces severe financial misallocation. Last-click attribution routinely over-credits bottom-funnel channels (such as brand paid search and affiliate coupon sites) while completely undervaluing high-impact upper-funnel investments (such as sports sponsorships, programmatic audio, television advertising, and organic social activations). Marketing budgets are channeled into defensive branded keywords while the top-of-funnel customer pipeline dries up.

To establish true commercial attribution and protect marketing efficiency, operators must implement [marketing ROI attribution solutions](/solutions/marketing-roi-attribution). By transitioning from individual tracking to econometric **Marketing Mix Modelling (MMM)** within [Jurnii Cortex](/products/jurnii-mmm), operators isolate true incremental lift and optimise capital allocation across every acquisition channel.

### 1. Adstock Transformations: Accounting for Lagged Impact

Advertising spend does not evaporate immediately after broadcast. A television commercial or podcast sponsorship builds cognitive brand equity that influences bettor behaviour for days or weeks into the future. 

MMM models apply mathematical **adstock decay functions** to capture the carry-over effect of media investments:

$$\text{Adstock}_t = \text{Spend}_t + \lambda \times \text{Adstock}_{t-1}$$

*(Where $\lambda$ represents the retention decay rate specific to each marketing channel).*

### 2. Diminishing Returns and Channel Saturation

Every marketing channel suffers from diminishing marginal returns. Doubling paid search spend will not double conversions once the core pool of high-intent keywords is exhausted. 

MMM models fit **Hill saturation curves** to calculate the exact point where additional spend in a channel produces marginal ROI below the cost of capital.

This ensures that an increase in FTDs during the World Cup is properly attributed to tournament seasonality rather than falsely credited to paid search ad copy.
