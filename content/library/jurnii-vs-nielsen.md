# Jurnii vs Nielsen MMM: what iGaming operators need to know

By Timothy Solomon

## The Short Version

- Nielsen MMM is the FMCG gold standard. Decades of pedigree, panel data, and credibility.
- iGaming variables (bonus mechanics, sporting calendars, regulatory shocks, competitor promo intensity) are not in their core model.
- For operators, that means coefficients on TV and digital that look fine in isolation but don't add up to a defensible P&L explanation.
- Cortex models those variables as first-class inputs.

## Why Nielsen exists

Nielsen MMM grew up in FMCG. The unit economics there are: a shopper buys a product at a retail price, you have a manufacturer-suggested price, you have promotion depth, and you have media spend. The model fits beautifully.

iGaming isn't that.

## The structural mismatch

In iGaming, there is no "shelf price." The product changes daily — through odds, boosts, free bets, reload offers, missions. Spend granularity matters in a way Nielsen's panel-based methodology doesn't naturally accommodate.

We've seen Nielsen models for operators with R² values that look defensible until you ask the model to explain a Cheltenham week — at which point the residuals double.

## Where Nielsen still adds value

Long-term brand-tracking. Their panel data is genuinely unique.

TV attribution at scale, especially for operators running broadcast across multiple territories.

And procurement comfort, which we won't pretend isn't real.

## When to choose Jurnii

When the questions you're asking are: "Is paid social saturated?" "Should we cut reload offers?" "What's the marginal ROI on the next pound into TV during Cheltenham?" — these are the questions Cortex was built to answer.
