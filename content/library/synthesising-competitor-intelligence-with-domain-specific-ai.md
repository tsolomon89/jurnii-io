### 1. Inability to Compute Promotional Mathematics

A generic AI model evaluates text semantically. When prompted with two competing casino offers:
- **Offer A**: "100% Deposit Match up to £100 (40x Wagering on Bonus)"
- **Offer B**: "Deposit £20, Get 50 Zero-Wagering Free Spins on Starburst (£0.10/spin)"

A generic LLM will frequently conclude that Offer A is more generous because the nominal figure (£100) is higher. A domain-trained model computes the underlying expected value (EV) against standard slot game RTP matrices, recognizing that Offer B delivers vastly higher player realisation and represents a far more aggressive commercial acquisition investment.

### 2. Lack of Jurisdiction-Specific Regulatory Context

Regulatory codes in jurisdictions such as the United Kingdom, Ontario, Germany, and the Netherlands impose strict constraints on advertising terms, bonus claiming conditions, and safer gambling disclosures. Generic models cannot distinguish between compliant market standard terms and regulatory infractions that expose operators to enforcement penalties.

### 3. Hallucination and Vague Consulting Clichés

When generic AI tools are asked to synthesize market trends, they default to repetitive, unevidenced generalisations. They produce paragraphs filled with filler phrases while failing to identify the exact market movements that move Net Gaming Revenue (NGR).
