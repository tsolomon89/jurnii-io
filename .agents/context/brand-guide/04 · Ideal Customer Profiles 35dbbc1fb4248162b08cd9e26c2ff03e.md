## ICP overview and hierarchy

Jurnii has two ICPs. Operators are primary — they are the commercial priority, the product is built for them, and all channel and content decisions default to reaching them. Enterprise suppliers are secondary — they are a strategic partner channel that has delivered clients and represents a route to embedded distribution.

**Why operators are primary:** The commercial pain is acute, the buying trigger is clear, and the ROI case is direct. Operators feel the CAC pressure, the player multi-homing, and the platform commoditisation every quarter. The problem Jurnii solves is their daily reality.

**Why suppliers are secondary (not abandoned):** 5 of Jurnii's first 17 clients came through partner introductions. Suppliers can embed Jurnii into their client ecosystem as a value-add, creating distribution that doesn't require direct commercial effort. But the product narrative and all content is built for operators — suppliers are a channel, not an audience.

## Primary ICP — operators

### Firmographic definition

- Multi-brand, multi-jurisdiction sportsbook and/or casino operators
- £50M+ revenue, or fast-scaling challengers with clear growth trajectory
- Operating in UK, EU, LATAM, or North America
- Using B2B platform stacks (Kambi, SBTech, Pragmatic, Pariplay, etc.) or hybrid models
- Minimum 3 brands — single-market, single-brand operators are a lower priority
- Regulated in at least one major market (UKGC, MGA, NJDGE, or equivalent)

### Commercial reality these operators face

- 70–80% of their players hold accounts at 3–5 competitors simultaneously
- 10% of players drive 80% of revenue — these players are the most sensitive to friction and the most valuable to retain
- Product differentiation is narrowing as B2B platform stacks converge
- CAC is rising while regulatory controls tighten promotional mechanics
- Manual competitor tracking does not scale at the volume of activity in the market
- Reactive analytics identify issues after the commercial damage is done

## Decision-maker profiles

### Chief Product Officer (CPO)

**Their world:** Responsible for the product experience across all brands and markets. Owns the UX roadmap, manages relationships with B2B platform vendors, and faces constant pressure to ship improvements faster than vendor timelines allow. Sits between commercial teams asking for features and engineering teams constrained by platform architecture.

**What they care about:** Evidence-based prioritisation. Competitive parity and differentiation. Reducing the influence of internal opinion on roadmap decisions. Understanding where their UX sits relative to competitors — not just internal metrics.

**Their language:** Journey effectiveness, conversion rate, funnel drop-off, registration flow, retention mechanics, platform constraints, vendor roadmap.

**What keeps them up at night:** Shipping the wrong thing. Being told by a competitor's UX that they're losing ground. Internal misalignment on what to build next.

**Best opening angle:** "You have 300+ brands to benchmark against — and right now you're doing it manually, or not at all."

### Chief Commercial Officer (CCO)

**Their world:** Owns revenue — acquisition, retention, promotion strategy, CRM, and trading. Accountable to the board for NGR. Manages a promotional budget that is under pressure from regulation and rising costs. Needs to know what competitors are doing in near-real-time to make promotional decisions that are competitive rather than reactively copied.

**What they care about:** Promotional incrementality. Competitive intelligence. Speed of reaction to competitor moves. Understanding whether their offers are leading or lagging the market.

**Their language:** NGR, GGR, promo cost, hold rate, boost frequency, free bet value, player lifetime value, market generosity.

**What keeps them up at night:** Waking up to find a competitor has launched a major acquisition offer they didn't know was coming. Spending promotional budget on mechanics that are already saturated in the market.

**Best opening angle:** "You're tracking 400+ monthly promotions across 5 UK competitors manually — or you're not tracking them at all."

### Chief Marketing Officer (CMO)

**Their world:** Manages acquisition channels, brand, and increasingly retention. Faces rising CPAs and needs to justify marketing spend with evidence. Responsible for brand positioning in a crowded market. Interested in how competitors are communicating and positioning their products.

**What they care about:** Marketing efficiency. Brand differentiation. Understanding what messaging is working in the market. Justifying budget to the board.

**Their language:** CPA, LTV, ROAS, attribution, brand equity, creative performance, media mix.

**What keeps them up at night:** Marketing budgets that don't generate sufficient return. Not being able to explain why acquisition metrics moved.

**Best opening angle:** "Jurnii 360 produces MMM-ready competitive data that explains performance variance against market activity — not just against your own historical baseline."

### Chief Executive Officer (CEO)

**Their world:** Accountable to the board and investors for overall commercial performance. Navigating regulatory change, M&A activity, and competitive pressure simultaneously. Wants the organisation to move faster and make better decisions.

**What they care about:** Competitive positioning. Defensible advantage. Organisational alignment around a shared commercial view. Board-ready data.

**Their language:** Market share, EBITDA, margin, player acquisition, regulatory risk, strategic differentiation.

**What keeps them up at night:** Being outmanoeuvred by a competitor while the organisation debates internally what to do.

**Best opening angle:** "The operators who win won't spend the most — they'll see the market most clearly. Jurnii is the infrastructure that compounds."

### Head of CRM / Retention

**Their world:** Responsible for keeping players active and returning. Works with data on player behaviour, promotional mechanics, and lifecycle journeys. Increasingly under pressure to retain players who are simultaneously receiving offers from 3–5 competitors.

**What they care about:** Churn prediction and prevention. Promotional timing and mechanics. Understanding what competitors are offering to their existing players. Loyalty and VIP programme effectiveness.

**Best opening angle:** "The retention journey is where most operators are least competitive — and where Jurnii's benchmarking data shows the biggest gaps."

### Head of UX / CX

**Their world:** Often lacks the organisational weight to push UX improvements up the priority list. Has opinions about what's wrong with the product but struggles to quantify them in commercial terms. Relies on heuristic audits and user research that are slow and expensive.

**What they care about:** Evidence-based advocacy. Being able to say "this friction point costs X in conversion" rather than "this doesn't feel right."

**Best opening angle:** "70+ commercially weighted UX recommendations, ranked by revenue impact, delivered in minutes — not weeks."

## Buying triggers

These are the events that move an operator from passive interest to active evaluation. Knowing these triggers allows outreach to be timed and framed correctly.

| Trigger | Signal to watch for | How to frame Jurnii |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market share stagnation | Quarterly results not moving despite spend increases | "You're optimising spend but not the experience that determines whether spend converts" |
| High promo cost, unclear incrementality | CCO asking hard questions about ROI of promotional budget | "360 gives you the market context to justify or challenge every promotional decision" |
| Leadership change (CPO, CMO, or CEO hire) | New exec announced publicly | New execs want to understand the market quickly — Jurnii is the fastest way |
| Regulatory shift forcing margin review | New UKGC guidance, affordability checks, bonus restrictions | "Margin compression means experience is the only remaining lever" |
| Competitive loss | Player or market share going to a specific competitor | "We can show you exactly what they're doing differently" |
| Platform migration or replatforming | Operator announces new platform or technology investment | Replatforming creates UX benchmarking need — what does "good" look like on the new stack? |

## Secondary ICP — enterprise suppliers & strategic partners

### Who they are

- B2B platform providers (front-end frameworks, sportsbook engines, casino platforms)
- CRM and player journey orchestration suppliers
- AI tooling and personalisation partners
- Payment and verification providers
- Affiliate and media networks with operator relationships

### Their pain

- Need differentiation beyond "modular" and "flexible" — these claims are commoditised
- Under pressure to show a meaningful AI and intelligence roadmap to retain operator clients
- Client operators are asking for real-time benchmarking and competitive intelligence that suppliers can't provide natively
- Need proof of commercial impact beyond platform uptime and feature delivery

### Partnership triggers

- Client operators requesting intelligence or benchmarking capability
- Need to embed AI meaningfully rather than as a feature label
- Seeking to expand wallet share with existing operator clients
- Wanting to differentiate their platform in competitive procurement processes

### How to frame Jurnii for suppliers

"Embed performance intelligence directly into your ecosystem. Give your operator clients the competitive intelligence layer they can't get elsewhere — delivered through your platform relationship."
