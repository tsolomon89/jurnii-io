## Why brand health metrics matter

For a business at Jurnii’s stage, the temptation is to measure only commercial outcomes — demos, pilots, clients. Those metrics matter. But they are lagging indicators of brand health. If brand positioning is working, you see the leading indicators first — and you can course-correct before the commercial metrics move.

The three categories below are ordered from leading to lagging: positioning signals come first, authority metrics follow, commercial brand metrics confirm.

---

## Positioning success signals

These are qualitative signals. They tell you whether the category definition and narrative pillars are landing with the right people.

**The strongest signal: prospect language mirroring**

When a prospect in a demo or first conversation uses the phrase “intelligence layer” or “proactive benchmarking” before Jurnii does — the positioning is working. Track this. Note it in CRM.

**Other key signals:**

- Inbound enquiries reference “proprietary benchmarking” or “competitive intelligence layer” unprompted
- Prospects describe Jurnii as infrastructure or a platform they want to build on — not a tool they want to try
- Media coverage uses Jurnii’s category language (“commercial intelligence”, “UX benchmarking layer”) rather than generic alternatives (“UX tool”, “analytics platform”)
- Speaking invitations are positioned in the intelligence/data/commercial strategy track — not the UX or design track
- Investors reference the intelligence layer framing and data moat argument in their own conversations

## Authority metrics

These measure Jurnii’s earned presence in the market. They are the output of consistent content and PR activity.

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Major industry media mentions per year | 10+ |
| Conference speaking slots per year | 3+ |
| Whitepapers or market reports cited externally | 2+ |
| LinkedIn combined exec reach (Fraser + TD + MV) | Growing month on month |
| LinkedIn engagement rate on content posts | 10%+ |
| Executive newsletter subscribers | 500+ |
| Inbound press enquiries per quarter | 3+ |

**How to interpret:**

Authority metrics confirm that the brand is being heard — not just that it’s spending. A business with 10+ industry media mentions per year is building ambient credibility that reduces friction in commercial conversations. A business with 0 is invisible between outbound touchpoints.

---

## Commercial brand metrics

These connect brand activity directly to revenue pipeline. They are the commercial proof that brand investment is working.

| Metric | Target | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demo-to-pilot conversion rate | 30%+ | Quality of brand credibility at demo stage |
| Inbound demo requests as % of total demos | Increasing quarter on quarter | Brand pull vs sales push |
| Time from first content touch to demo request | Track and trend | Content-to-pipeline efficiency |
| New demos citing a specific piece of content | Track | Which content is driving commercial action |
| Qualified demos per month | 60+ annually | Pipeline volume |

**Important distinction:**

These are *brand* metrics, not just sales metrics. The specific measurements here — inbound rate, time from content touch, content citation — are tracking the brand’s commercial efficiency, not just the sales team’s activity.

## Document review cadence

### Quarterly review

**What to check:**

- Are the positioning claims in Chapters 2 and 3 still accurate given market developments?
- Are the proof points in Chapter 5 current? (300+ brands, 70+ recommendations, 400+ monthly promotions)
- Has any new client, product capability, or market data emerged that strengthens the framework?
- Are the objection responses in Chapter 7 still handling the most common objections, or have new ones emerged?

**Who reviews:** Fraser + Chloe

**Output:** List of specific updates required, if any

### Annual full review

**What to assess:**

- Does the category definition still hold? Has a competitor attempted to claim the same category?
- Do the ICPs still reflect who is actually buying, or has the buyer profile shifted?
- Does the tone of voice still match how the company actually communicates in its most effective conversations?
- Are the narrative pillars still the right stories for the current market conditions?

**Who reviews:** Full founding team + key commercial hires

**Output:** Updated document version. Version logged in Notion. All Claude project contexts updated.

## Maintaining Claude context

This framework is loaded as project context for Claude tasks. When the document is updated, the project context must be updated simultaneously.

**Chapters that Claude draws on most frequently:**

- Chapter 1 — purpose and values (every task)
- Chapter 3 — narrative pillars (all content tasks)
- Chapter 4 — ICPs (all outreach and persona-specific tasks)
- Chapter 5 — product messaging (all product copy tasks)
- Chapter 6 — tone of voice and vocabulary (all content tasks)
- Chapter 7 — objection handling (all sales tasks)

**Minimum context for a Claude content task:** Chapters 1, 3, 4 (relevant ICP), 5 (relevant product), 6.

**Additional context for sales tasks:** Chapter 7.

**If outputs feel generic:** The most common cause is that the voice chapter (Chapter 6) is not in context, or the vocabulary rules are being bypassed. Re-run with Chapter 6 loaded and check the output against the banned terms list.
