## Brand personality traits

Five traits define how Jurnii shows up. Each trait has a behavioural definition — what it looks like in practice — and an exclusion — what it rules out.

### Strategic

Jurnii connects every observation directly to commercial consequence. We do not comment on design or UX in isolation. Every finding, every piece of content, every claim maps to revenue, margin, churn, or conversion.

**Rules out:** Design commentary without commercial framing. Observations that are interesting but not actionable. The word "insight" when there is no implication for what to do next.

### Commercially literate

Jurnii speaks the language of the CFO as fluently as the language of the CPO. We use NGR, ARPU, CAC, LTV, churn, and hold rate — not just "user experience" and "engagement."

**Rules out:** UX softness. Design awards framing. Engagement metrics that don't connect to revenue. Any framing that would only resonate with a design team and not a commercial team.

### Objective

Jurnii replaces opinion with structured benchmarking. We do not say something "feels wrong" — we show what the data says. We do not make claims we cannot evidence. When we have a strong point of view, we show our working.

**Rules out:** Gut-feel commentary. Style preferences presented as expertise. Consulting language that sounds authoritative but isn't evidenced. "Best practice" without a benchmark.

### Forward-looking

Jurnii is always framing the next move, not the last one. We talk about what's coming in the market, what operators should be doing now, and what the intelligence reveals about where things are going — not what happened last quarter.

**Rules out:** Retrospective reporting for its own sake. Analysis that concludes without a commercial implication. Historical data presented without a forward recommendation.

### Calm authority

Jurnii is confident without being loud. We do not shout. We do not use hype language. We do not chase the news cycle. The intelligence speaks — we do not need to dramatise it.

**Rules out:** AI superlatives. Hype language. Exclamation marks. Drama. Urgency manufactured by marketing rather than grounded in genuine commercial consequence.

**A senior commercial strategist who has seen every cycle, trusts data over opinion, and is interested only in what moves the business — never in sounding clever.**

## Tone rules — write like this

These are the positive rules. Each comes with a before/after example.

### Specific claims with numbers

Generic claims are noise. Specific claims with numbers are evidence.

> ❌ "Jurnii helps operators understand their competitive position."
> 

> ✅ "Jurnii tracks 400+ monthly promotions across 5 UK operators — automatically."
> 

### Short sentences under pressure

When making a strong commercial point, the sentence should be short. Length dilutes force.

> ❌ "Jurnii provides a range of analytical capabilities that can help commercial teams better understand what their competitors are doing in the market."
> 

> ✅ "Jurnii shows you what competitors are launching. In near-real-time. Every day."
> 

### Commercial language

Speak in the language of the people making decisions.

> ❌ "Improve the user experience and reduce drop-off."
> 

> ✅ "Reduce registration drop-off by identifying the friction points costing you FTDs."
> 

### Active voice, present tense

Active constructions and present tense create momentum. Passive constructions and past tense create distance.

> ❌ "Competitor data can be tracked by operators using our platform."
> 

> ✅ "Jurnii tracks every competitor promotion. Automatically. Daily."
> 

### One idea per sentence

Complex sentences hide the argument. One idea per sentence forces clarity.

> ❌ "By providing continuous, automated benchmarking across UX and proposition, Jurnii enables operators to make faster, more commercially grounded decisions while reducing their dependence on manual research and subjective opinion."
> 

> ✅ "Jurnii automates benchmarking. Continuously. Across UX and proposition. That means faster decisions — grounded in evidence, not opinion."
> 

### Friction named without drama

When naming a problem, be specific and factual — not dramatic.

> ❌ "Operators are flying blind in a fiercely competitive market."
> 

> ✅ "Most operators review competitor activity quarterly. The market moves daily."
>

## Tone rules — never do this

### AI superlatives

Never: "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "groundbreaking", "transformative", "cutting-edge", "next-generation"

Why: These words have been used by every AI product for five years. They signal that the writer couldn't think of a specific claim. They erode credibility immediately with commercially literate buyers.

Instead: Make a specific claim about what Jurnii actually does differently.

### UX softness

Never: "Delightful", "intuitive", "seamless", "frictionless" (when referring to Jurnii's own output), "user-centric"

Why: These are design-world terms that mean nothing to a CCO or CEO. They also position Jurnii as a UX agency — exactly the wrong category.

Instead: Frame the UX finding in commercial terms.

### "AI-powered" used alone

Never say "Jurnii is AI-powered" without specifying what the AI does.

Why: Every product claims to be AI-powered. The claim is meaningless without specificity.

Instead: "Jurnii uses AI to analyse 300+ brands across four UX categories and rank improvements by commercial impact."

### Passive constructions

Never: "Insights are generated", "data is collected", "recommendations are provided"

Instead: "Jurnii generates", "Jurnii collects", "Jurnii delivers"

### Consulting filler

Never: "Leverage", "synergies", "holistic", "end-to-end", "best-in-class", "ecosystem", "robust"

These words are free — they cost nothing and mean nothing. Replace with the specific thing being described.

### Hedging language

Never: "Could potentially", "may help", "might be useful for", "in some cases"

Jurnii makes specific, evidenced claims. If a claim needs hedging, the claim is wrong.

## Vocabulary — own these terms

Consistent use of these terms builds brand recognition. When Jurnii uses them and competitors don't, the terms become associated with Jurnii.

| Term | Definition | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intelligence layer** | The strategic framing for what Jurnii is — infrastructure, not a tool | Always use when describing Jurnii at a category level |
| **Proprietary benchmarking** | Benchmarking built on Jurnii's own dataset of 300+ brands, not scraped or third-party data | Use when explaining why Jurnii's analysis is different from generic tools |
| **Proactive UX** | UX intelligence that identifies friction before it affects revenue | Use when contrasting with reactive analytics |
| **Commercial visibility** | What an operator gains from Jurnii — a clear view of their market position | Use as the benefit frame |
| **Near real-time** | The temporal quality of Jurnii 360's intelligence — hours not days | Use specifically for 360. Do not claim real-time if there is a processing delay |
| **Experience intelligence** | The category of intelligence Jurnii provides — broader than UX audit, narrower than general analytics | Use as the category descriptor when "commercial intelligence layer" is too specific for the context |
| **Always-on intelligence** | Continuous vs snapshot — the structural advantage of Jurnii over manual and agency alternatives | Use when contrasting with quarterly reviews or ad-hoc audits |
| **Promo Richness Index** | Jurnii's proprietary metric for quantifying promotional generosity | Capitalise. Use as a brand asset, not just a feature |
| **Commercial decision enablement** | What Jurnii does for operators — enables commercial decisions that are faster and more defensible | Use in B2B positioning and thought leadership |

## Vocabulary — never say

| Banned term | Why | Use instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "AI-powered" (alone) | Meaningless without specificity | Describe what the AI actually does |
| "End-to-end solution" | Consulting filler with no meaning | Describe what Jurnii actually covers |

| "Game-changing" | Same as revolutionary | Make a specific claim |

| "Holistic" | Consulting filler | Describe the specific scope |
| "Robust" | Meaningless qualifier | Remove it entirely |

| "Ecosystem" | Overused tech word | Describe the actual network or set of products |
| "Best-in-class" | Unsubstantiated superlative | Use a specific benchmark claim |

## Tone by channel

The voice is consistent. The register adapts.

| Channel | Register | Length | Formality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn posts | Direct, data-led or opinion-led. Peer to peer. | 150–200 words | Low-medium |
| Press releases | Factual, structured, quote-led | 300–500 words | Medium-high |
| Sales emails | Short, specific, single ask | Under 80 words | Medium |
| Demo conversations | Conversational, evidence-based, not scripted | N/A | Low-medium |
| Investor updates | Milestone-led, metrics-first, narrative second | Under 200 words | Medium |
| Website copy | Clear, specific, CTA on every section | Varies by page | Medium |
| Conference abstracts | Problem → data insight → commercial implication | Under 300 words | Medium-high |
| Thought leadership articles | Single argument, deeply evidenced | 700–1,000 words | Medium |
