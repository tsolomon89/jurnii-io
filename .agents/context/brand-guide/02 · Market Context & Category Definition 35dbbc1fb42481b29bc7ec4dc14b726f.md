## The iGaming commercial reality

The market conditions Jurnii was built for are structural — they are not temporary trends. They are the operating environment every regulated operator now faces.

**Rising customer acquisition cost (CAC)**

Acquisition costs continue to increase as digital channels saturate and regulatory constraints on bonus and promotional mechanics tighten. Operators must optimise the full commercial system — not just buy more traffic. The margin for waste in the customer journey is contracting.

**Player multi-homing**

70–80% of UK players hold accounts with 3–5 operators simultaneously. They switch instantly. One poor experience is not recovered from — the player simply uses a competitor. The 10% of players who drive 80% of revenue are the most likely to multi-home and the most sensitive to friction.

**Platform commoditisation**

The majority of regulated operators run on a small number of B2B platform stacks. They share the same game library, the same payment infrastructure, and increasingly the same roadmap. If five operators run on the same stack, they are competing on reskin, not product. Differentiation cannot wait on vendor roadmaps.

**Regulatory margin compression**

Regulatory changes across UK, EU, and emerging markets continue to compress promotional flexibility and tighten the conditions under which operators can acquire and retain players. The operators who survive margin compression are the ones who optimise experience rather than outspend the market.

**AI reshaping interaction**

LLMs are becoming interface layers. Players will increasingly interact with operators through AI-native experiences. Operators who have proprietary intelligence infrastructure are better positioned to adapt — operators relying on generic AI tools are adding noise, not signal.

## The category Jurnii owns

**Jurnii is a proprietary commercial intelligence layer.**

This is not a product category that existed before Jurnii — it is a category Jurnii is defining. The framing matters strategically:

- A **product** is bought and used. A **layer** is built on. Layers become infrastructure. Infrastructure creates switching costs and structural dependency.
- Operators do not buy a commercial intelligence layer for a quarter and cancel it. After 12 months of longitudinal benchmark data, leaving means losing the entire historical record. That is not a renewal decision — it is a structural dependency.
- The category framing also positions Jurnii correctly against both agency alternatives (which are services, not infrastructure) and analytics tools (which are reporting layers, not intelligence layers).

When speaking about Jurnii, always use "intelligence layer" — not "platform", not "tool", not "solution." The category word is load-bearing.

## The defensibility case

Jurnii gets harder to displace every month. This is the argument for investors and for enterprise-level commercial conversations.

**Data moat**

Every audit cycle adds a data point to a time series no competitor can buy or replicate after the fact. 12 months of benchmark data across 80+ brands is already ahead of any new entrant by 12 months — and that gap compounds. The dataset is proprietary and grows continuously.

**Multi-product intelligence combination**

UX quality data, promotional intelligence, player expectation modelling, market risk signals, and marketing spend context in a single intelligence model. No competitor offers all five dimensions. The combination creates switching costs that individual point products do not.

**Structural dependency at enterprise scale**

Once an operator group has 12 months of Jurnii longitudinal data embedded in their planning cycle, leaving means losing their entire historical benchmark. At enterprise contract scale, this is not a renewal decision — it is a structural dependency. This is the correct frame for enterprise procurement conversations.

**Regional coverage expansion**

As Jurnii signs more clients and builds out data sets, regional coverage expands — providing the kind of jurisdiction-specific intelligence typically only available to global operators or large agencies. This additional lens compounds the data moat.
