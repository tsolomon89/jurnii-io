# 09 · Content & Channel Guidance

> How the brand shows up across every channel. This chapter tells Claude which format to use for which task, and tells employees what quality looks like for each content type. Every piece of content Jurnii publishes should be traceable to a demo request.
>

## Content principles

Three rules that apply to every piece of content Jurnii publishes. If a piece of content cannot satisfy all three, it should not be published.

**1. Every piece must have a direct path to a demo request.**

Content is a pipeline tool, not a brand exercise. The metric for content quality is not engagement — it is demo requests. If a piece of content cannot plausibly move a reader toward requesting a demo, it is a lower priority.

**2. Always derived from data or direct experience — never from a blank page.**

No content should be created as an original creative exercise. Every post, article, or pitch derives from: platform data, a Jurnii market report, a media placement, an event observation, or a founding team's direct market experience. Blank-page content reads as generic. Evidence-derived content reads as authoritative.

**3. Commercial benefit must be explicit — not implied.**

The reader should not have to infer why the content matters commercially. State it. If a post discusses a UX finding, end with the commercial consequence. If an article covers a market trend, include what operators should do about it.

## LinkedIn (exec team)

### Structure of a strong Jurnii LinkedIn post

1. **Hook** — a single sentence that creates a reason to stop scrolling. Data-led (a specific number that surprises) or opinion-led (a direct, slightly contrarian statement).
2. **Development** — 2–3 short paragraphs expanding the argument. One idea per paragraph.
3. **Commercial implication** — what should an operator do with this information?
4. **Close** — a question, a data point, or a soft CTA ("If you want to see how your platform compares...")

### Rules

- 150–200 words. Not longer.
- No post should be written from a blank page. Always identify the source material first.
- No hashtag spam. Maximum 2 relevant hashtags per post.
- No promotional posts. Posts that read as adverts will be ignored or hidden by the algorithm and damage personal brand credibility.
- Every post must pass the credibility test: would a CPO or CCO at a Tier 1 operator find this useful or interesting?

### Hook scoring

Before publishing, score the hook 0–100 on: specificity (does it contain a number or a named entity?), surprise (would a senior operator stop at this?), relevance (does it map to an ICP pain point?). Target 70+.

**Strong hooks:**

> "70% of iGaming players hold accounts with 4+ operators. Here’s what that means for your retention strategy."
> 

> "We’ve analysed 300+ betting products. The registration flow is where the most revenue is being lost — and most operators don’t know it."
> 

**Weak hooks:**

> "Exciting news from the Jurnii team!"
> 

> "We’ve been thinking about UX in iGaming..."

## Market intelligence reports

### Standard report structure

1. **Methodology note** — brief (2–3 sentences). What was analysed, over what period, across which brands. Establishes credibility.
2. **Key findings** — 3–5 findings, each with a specific data point and a commercial implication.
3. **Market context** — what broader trends does this data connect to?
4. **Commercial implications** — what should operators do with this information? Specific, not generic.
5. **Jurnii CTA** — soft, specific. Not “contact us.” “If you want to see how your promotional strategy compares to this data, request a demo.”

### Cadence

Quarterly. Timed to major sporting events or industry moments (Cheltenham, Grand National, World Cup, Euros, ICE, SBC).

### Distribution

Primary: Direct outreach to named accounts and warm prospects (the report is the outreach trigger, not a product pitch).

Secondary: PR pitch to relevant trade publications.

Tertiary: LinkedIn distribution via exec team.

## Case studies

### Structure

- **Situation** — what was the client’s commercial challenge before Jurnii?
- **Task** — what did they need Jurnii to do?
- **Action** — specifically what did Jurnii do and how?
- **Result** — what changed commercially? Specific metrics where permitted.

### Standards

- 400–600 words
- One pull quote from the client, signed off before publication
- All metrics and claims client-approved before publication
- Dual format: web version for Webflow, PDF version for sales distribution
- Never published without explicit client approval

### Framing rules

- Frame the client’s outcome without overpromising for future clients
- Use the client’s commercial language, not Jurnii’s internal framing
- The result section must contain at least one specific metric or quantified outcome

## PR & thought leadership

### Pitch format

Under 100 words. Subject line + 3-sentence body.

**Subject line formula:** [Specific data point or market observation] + [publication context]

**Body structure:**

1. The specific insight or data point (one sentence)
2. Why it matters commercially to their readership (one sentence)
3. What Fraser/Jurnii can offer (one sentence) + contact

### Conference abstract formula

Market problem → Jurnii data insight → commercial implication for operators.

Under 300 words. Include a suggested session title. Always submit 3–6 months before the event.

**Approved target publications:** iGaming Business, EGR, SiGMA News, [Casino.org](http://Casino.org), Talk Gambling, Gambling Insider, iGaming Next

## Outreach emails

### Rules

- Under 80 words
- Content-first entry: always lead with a relevant data point, market observation, or piece of Jurnii research — never lead with a product description
- Single ask: a 20-minute demo
- Tone: peer-to-peer, not vendor-to-prospect
- No generic openers: "I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "I’m getting in touch about"

### Structure

1. The specific hook (1 sentence — what makes this relevant to them, right now)
2. The specific Jurnii mechanism that solves it (1–2 sentences)
3. The single ask (1 sentence)

### Example

> We’ve just tracked 400+ promotions across the UK market in the past month. Bet365 ran 3x more daily boosts than the market average. Happy to show you how [their company] compares in a 20-minute demo — does [day] work?

## Website copy standards

### Hero copy structure

1. Headline — makes an argument, not a label. The category claim.
2. Sub-headline — the mechanism (how Jurnii does what the headline claims)
3. Social proof — one specific metric or client reference
4. CTA — specific, not generic. “See how your platform compares” not “Learn more.”

### Product page structure

1. Product positioning statement (one line)
2. The problem it solves (specific, commercial)
3. How it works (mechanism — not feature list)
4. Proof points (3 specific metrics)
5. CTA

### ICP landing pages

Each ICP landing page (CPO, CCO, CMO) should:

- Lead with their specific pain, in their language
- Explain Jurnii’s mechanism for solving it
- Include one relevant proof point
- End with a specific demo ask relevant to their role

### Meta standards

- Meta title: 55–60 characters. Include primary keyword + Jurnii.
- Meta description: 145–155 characters. Include the specific commercial benefit and a soft CTA.
- Every page: demo CTA visible without scrolling.
