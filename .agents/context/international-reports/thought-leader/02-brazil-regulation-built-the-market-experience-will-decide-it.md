# Regulation Built the Market. Experience Will Decide It.

### Brazil's regulated launch created 17.7 million bettors, 78 licensed operators, and a set of shared engines. When everyone runs the same core and every player holds five accounts, the sportsbook stops being the differentiator. The journey becomes it.

---

## The argument in one line

In Brazil, the odds engine, the live streaming, and the payment rail are increasingly common across operators. The only thing an operator fully controls is the experience on top, and in a market where the average bettor holds three to five accounts, that experience decides the deposit.

---

## Why now

Brazil regulated, and the market arrived faster than anyone modelled. There were 17.7 million unique bettors in the first half of 2025, over 78 licensed operators, and a top five holding roughly 47% of a fragmented market. That means the majority of volume is contested every day, by everyone, for the same wallets.

Three structural facts define how that contest is decided.

**The infrastructure is shared.** A large share of the licensed set runs on the same B2B cores. One provider powers the sportsbook for multiple direct competitors. A global group has deployed its proprietary engine, built for the UK and US, into the market at a stated cost of over $350m. When the odds, the live product, and the core betting infrastructure are common, structural differentiation cannot come from the engine. It has to come from interface, journey, and friction.

**The player multi-homes by default.** The Brazilian bettor holds three to five active accounts. Deposit share is decided in the moment, on the offer and the experience in front of them. Players do not churn loudly here. They reduce wallet share silently, across four apps at once. The operator with the smoother deposit and the clearer journey wins the next session, and the months of value that follow it.

**The margin is tightening before the market has matured.** GGR tax is rising on a published schedule, and a deposit-tax mechanism has passed the Senate. As a single-market operator, a Brazilian pure-play has no geographic diversification to absorb the compression. Every point of GGR spent on promotion has to be justified against return, which makes promotional efficiency, and the experience that converts it, a commercial necessity rather than a preference.

There is one more number that reframes the whole contest. Channelisation into the regulated market sits near half; a large illegal market still competes for the same players. In that environment, experience quality is not only how operators win share from each other. It is how the regulated market keeps players inside the regulated market at all.

## Evidence 1: The engine is common; the scores are not

If the sportsbook engine decided outcomes, operators sharing a core would converge on experience. They do not. Jurnii UX evaluates the licensed Brazilian set across four dimensions and 400-plus data points per brand, rolled into a single benchmarked score. The spread across the top of the market is 11 points, and the operators sit in a different order than their engines or their marketing budgets would predict.

| Position in the licensed set | Jurnii UX score | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Top of set | 74 | Highest journey scores |
| Strong foundation | 73 | Close behind on UX |
| Consistent across surfaces | 71 | Registration edge |
| Scale-led, largest share | 67 | Biggest brand, not best journey |
| Global engine deploying | 65 | Improving as the platform lands |
| Global template ported | 64 | Late entrant |
| Shared-engine, CX-invested | 63 | Mid-pack, room to move |

The scale leader, the brand with the largest tracked share, sits mid-table on experience. The operator with a globally proven engine sits below three domestic-facing brands. The engine is not the moat. The journey is. And the journey is where the score gap between these brands actually opens: casino discovery, deposit flow, registration, and getting help.

---

## Evidence 2: Regulation put the friction exactly where the money is

Brazil's post-regulation KYC requirements introduce verification friction at the point of highest intent, the first deposit. This is not an edge case. It is the single most important commercial moment in the funnel, and regulation guarantees there is friction inside it. The competitive question is not whether an operator's onboarding has friction. Every operator's does. It is whether that flow handles the friction better than the operator in the next tab.

The market's answers diverge sharply. Registration friction, measured as effective steps to activation, runs from a single-screen form at one operator to a three-step flow *plus mandatory facial recognition* at another, the highest onboarding friction in the cohort, imposed at the exact moment a new player is deciding whether to continue. Some brands surface the national tax ID at the very start and pre-fill name, date of birth, and address from it, cutting manual entry to almost nothing. Others ask for it late, as free text, with no format guidance. One operator's withdrawal flow gates behind a biometric check with no documented recovery path, a tested failure that locked the user out entirely.

Every one of these is a deposit-moment decision, and none of them is visible in a promotional report. In a market where the player has four alternatives open, the operator with the cleaner first-deposit journey does not win a preference. It wins the deposit.

## Evidence 3: The World Cup is an acquisition event that only pays back on retention

Brazil hosts the most concentrated acquisition window in its short regulated history. Exclusive tournament coverage on a single streaming platform carries 104 World Cup matches; that platform set a record of 5.6 million concurrent viewers. Three of the largest operators sponsor the same broadcast. The sponsorship asset is shared. The execution is not.

This is where the multi-homing market punishes awareness that does not convert. A 5.6-million-viewer moment is brand exposure, not retention. The Brazilian bettor will see four operators' World Cup creative before deciding where to deposit, and the sharpest, best-timed offer, landing on the cleanest journey, captures the deposit and the months of value behind it. Awareness bought at tournament prices and squandered on a friction-heavy first bet is a marketing cost line, not a marketing return.

The regulatory backdrop raises the stakes further. Senate-approved advertising restrictions, a watershed and a ban on live-broadcast betting ads, increase the marginal cost of brand-building precisely as the biggest brand-building window arrives. As broadcast tightens, the operators who win will be the ones whose product and promotional execution compensate for it. That is a shift from media buying to experience execution, and it is happening in real time.

---

## Evidence 4: Localisation is a moat with an expiry date

The domestic-facing brands lead the Brazilian experience benchmark for a reason. Hyper-local execution, Portuguese-language support, PIX-first payments that pre-fill from the national ID, Brazilian football partnerships, is a genuine differentiator today. It is also time-limited. Global operators are closing those gaps quickly, and the payment layer shows how quickly the ground can move: in one competitive audit, a global brand shipped a single PIX entry point where a domestic rival offered eight bank-specific variants and quick-select chips. Payment breadth at the deposit moment is not a back-office detail. It is the difference between a completed deposit and an abandoned one.

The operators treating localisation as a permanent advantage are misreading it. It is a lead measured in quarters, and the only way to defend a lead you cannot see eroding is to benchmark it continuously against the set that is closing it.

---

## What this means commercially

Brazil rewards a specific discipline: treat the journey as the product, and measure it against the market continuously.

**For product.** The engine is shared, so the roadmap has to be built on where the *journey* is breaking relative to competitors, not on what feels broken internally. Registration, first-deposit KYC, casino discovery, and help are where the score gap opens. Those are the surfaces that convert acquisition into retained value.

**For commercial and CRM.** In a market where players hold five accounts and the World Cup floods the funnel with casual registrants, retention is a journey question before it is a promotional one. The operator that wins the first cashout, not just the first deposit, keeps the player. That is measurable, and most operators are measuring only their own side of it.

**For leadership.** Channelisation sitting near half means the regulated market's growth ceiling is defined partly by how good the regulated experience is. Better journeys do not only move share between licensed operators. They keep players regulated. That reframes experience investment from a marketing expense to a market-development one.

Regulation built the Brazilian market. It also guaranteed that the market cannot be won on the engine, because the engine is common. What is left is the experience, and the experience is where the next decade of Brazilian share will be decided.
