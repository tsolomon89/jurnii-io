# UK World Cup 2026 Build-up — Boost Generosity vs Margin (Jurnii 360, Issue 01)

**Thesis:** A bigger headline boost ≠ better odds. Most operators are protecting margin while flying the World Cup flag. **Entain (Ladbrokes + Coral) is the exception** — the only books offering genuinely better odds, not just bigger numbers.

## Headline metrics (build-up window)
| Metric | Value | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|
| WC activations | **86** (70 boosts · 16 promos) | from zero in early May |
| WC median boost | **15.38%** | ▲ vs 14.29% football baseline — bigger sticker |
| WC margin conceded | **1.58pp** | ▼ vs 1.67pp baseline — **no better price** |
| WC Share-of-Voice leader | **William Hill 36.2%** | first live 15 May |

WC boosts more than doubled from week of 25 May into partial week of 1 June — before the draw/fixtures.

## Volume is not voice
On raw count, William Hill leads (37 of 86 activations, ~43%). But Jurnii 360's intensity-weighted Share of Voice (weights by richness + time live) tells a different story:

| Operator | Share of activations (count) | SoPV (intensity-weighted) |
|---|---|---|
| William Hill | 43.0% | 36.2% |
| Spreadex | 3.5% | 14.4% |
| bet365 | 18.6% | 11.9% |
| BoyleSports | 3.5% | 9.2% |
| BetVictor | 1.2% | 6.7% |
| Ladbrokes | 4.7% | 4.6% |
| Coral | 4.7% | 4.5% |

- **bet365** drops from 18.6% count to 11.9% voice — almost all single-day boosts.
- **Spreadex** ranks #2 on voice off just 3 activations — its WC welcome offers run ~22 days each (most efficient book on voice).

## Boost benchmark (vs 3-month football baseline, 1 Mar–31 May, 28,000+ boosts incl. Cheltenham/Aintree)
Median boost % per operator, football baseline → World Cup:

| Operator | Football baseline | World Cup | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market average | 14.3% | 15.4% | slightly bigger sticker, leaner price |
| Coral | 13.6% | **41.4%** | much more generous |
| Ladbrokes | 15.4% | **36.7%** | much more generous |
| Unibet | 18.2% | 22.2% | up |
| Sky Bet | 33.3% | 20.0% | leaner |
| William Hill | 16.7% | 15.4% | leaner than own norm |
| bet365 | 14.3% | 9.5% | much leaner |

## William Hill pulled back (biggest behaviour change)
- Feb baseline: conservative ~10% median boost.
- Apr–May peak: 18–20% median, conceding **3.0–3.6pp margin** (~3× bet365) — a sustained shift, not a one-off.
- World Cup: **15.38% median, 1.58pp margin** — below its own 3-month baseline (16.67%) and well short of recent 18–20%. 36 WC boosts, 97% via standard price-boost mechanic — volume without price.
- **bet365 pulled back harder:** 14.29% → 9.54% (nearly 5pp below football baseline).

## Entain is the exception (Ladbrokes + Coral, shared trading desk)
Only books to go genuinely aggressive on **both** numbers that matter (bigger headline AND more margin conceded = real value):
- **Coral:** median WC boost 41.43% (vs 13.64% baseline, +27.8pp) at **2.66pp margin conceded** (vs 1.58pp market norm).
- **Ladbrokes:** 36.66% median (+21.3pp on 15.38% baseline) at **4.76pp margin conceded** (~3× market average).
- Standout offers: Ladbrokes 50% on England to win all 3 group games + reach QF; Coral 43% on Harry Kane for Golden Ball, 43% on Messi as top scorer.
- This is "a book buying tournament share," not badging it.

## What to watch
1. Shift from tournament-wide outrights to **fixture-by-fixture boosts** (currently zero) — marks awareness → turnover.
2. **Generosity trend** — WC boosts are leaner than normal football today; any sustained rise in boost % AND margin conceded signals a book buying share for real.

*Takeaway: loud and generous are not the same thing; you need the right measure (margin conceded, not headline %) to tell them apart. Source: Jurnii 360 World Cup 2026 Promotional Tracker, Issue 01.*
