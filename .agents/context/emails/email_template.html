New in Jurnii 360: The Market Richness Index

The Market Richness Index (MRI) quantifies how generous the promotional market is at any point in time. It combines offer value, frequency, mechanic quality, and breadth of event coverage into a single 0 to 100 score, giving you one defensible, comparable metric for promotional generosity across any competitive set.

The score measures generosity relative to the market, not absolute spend. An operator scoring 81 sits in the top quartile for generosity that week. A score below 50 means the operator is materially less generous than peers on the factors that drive player choice.

MRI supports week-on-week tracking of overall market generosity as well as operator-level benchmarking. This enables commercial conversations grounded in data, not assumption.

Why MRI Changes the Commercial Conversation

Most operators assess promotional competitiveness through manual competitor checks and gut-feel conversations. The result: commercial teams either overspend to "match the market" or underspend and lose player share, both without data to justify the decision.

The Market Richness Index removes this guesswork. By combining offer value, frequency, mechanic quality, and event coverage into one 0 to 100 score, MRI enables conversations like: "We scored 47 last week while the market average was 62, so we need to increase generosity on Premier League accumulators, not across the board."

That specificity is the difference between commercial decision enablement and promotional noise. MRI is available now inside Jurnii 360.

 

World Cup Promotions: What Jurnii 360 Data Reveals

This month, Jurnii published exclusive analysis in EGR on how UK operators are actually using the 2026 World Cup window. The data comes directly from Jurnii 360, and it challenges several assumptions about tournament strategy.

Key findings from our EGR analysis:

 

• Retention, not acquisition. Of 125 genuine World Cup activations across 17 operators, 116 target existing customers and just 8 target new ones. For an event routinely described as the acquisition moment of the cycle, almost nobody is using it that way.

• Three distinct promotional layers. Activity splits into sportsbook (price boosts, free bets, money back), casino cross-sell (prize giveaways), and free-to-play (prediction games). An operator's World Cup strategy is a decision about which layers it leans on, and how heavily.

• The headline held, the value tightened. The median World Cup boost sat at 20% every single tournament week. But margin conceded peaked in the group stage at 2.8 percentage points, then fell to 1.8 in the knockouts. The shop window stayed fixed while the real value behind it narrowed.

• Boosts concentrated, not declined. As the fixture list collapsed into the knockouts, boosts per fixture climbed from 9 at kickoff to 36 in the knockout rounds. Every book converged on the same handful of games. Canada v South Africa drew 121 boosts across 12 books. England v Mexico drew 112 across 13.
